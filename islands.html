Three islands, three futures — tap each one

Britain's islands hit the mainland's energy problems decades early — too far out, too expensive to connect, left to solve it themselves. What they built looks remarkably like where the whole country is now heading.

A day on Eigg — try living under the cap

On Eigg, every household lives with a 5 kW limit — go over it and your supply trips, and you walk to the generation shed to get reconnected. Toggle the appliances and see how quickly an ordinary evening approaches the line. This is demand flexibility not as an app notification but as a way of life.

Why this matters beyond the islands
Everything this series has covered at national scale — curtailed wind, full cables, storage limits, demand flexibility — the islands lived first, at one ten-thousandth the size. They are not quaint outliers. They are the grid of 2035, already running, with the bugs being found by people who know each other's names.

What the mainland is quietly borrowing

Live limits
From Orkney
Orkney's cable to the mainland filled up years ago — so instead of refusing new wind, engineers built one of the world's first "active network management" systems: generators connect with a live, flexible limit instead of waiting years for reinforcement. The connections reform of Post 1 and the flexible connections now offered across GB are this idea, scaled up ten-thousand-fold.
Storage as a citizen
From Eigg
Eigg's batteries are not a market participant chasing price spreads — they are the thing that keeps the lights on when the hydro burn slows. Watching a community treat storage as shared infrastructure rather than a trading asset is a preview of arguments the mainland will have about who batteries actually serve.
Making fuel from surplus
From the northern isles
When you cannot export surplus electricity, you turn it into something else. Orkney has trialled using excess wind to make hydrogen for ferries and harbour use — the same soak-up-the-surplus logic behind the national storage and hydrogen questions of Posts 13 and 23, tested first where the cable ran out.
