What nuclear is actually for
Nuclear is not there to be cheap. It is there to be on — through the dark, still fortnight when wind, solar and batteries all fall short at once. It is the most expensive insurance policy Britain buys, against the one week a year when nothing else works.
Figure 03

The giant and the promise

Two bets running in parallel: finish the giants, and hope the small ones live up to the brochure. Honest numbers for both.

Hinkley Point C
3.2 GW · Somerset · under construction
Likely the most expensive object ever built in Britain: estimates around £46bn, roughly a decade late against the original promise, first power expected around the end of this decade. When running, it will power ~6 million homes for 60 years — and the lessons learned are meant to make its twin cheaper.
Sizewell C
3.2 GW · Suffolk · approved 2025
A copy of Hinkley, deliberately — same design, same supply chain, the theory being that the second one is always cheaper than the first. Final investment decision taken in 2025 at around £38bn with the state as a major shareholder. The 2030s will test the replication theory.
Small Modular Reactors
~0.5 GW each · Rolls-Royce selected 2025
The promise: factory-built reactors, assembled on site, costs falling with every unit — nuclear that behaves like a product instead of a megaproject. The honest status: none operating in the West yet, first UK units targeted for the mid-2030s. Plausible, important, unproven.
