What the series was really about
Twenty posts later, the thesis is simple: the energy transition is not a technology story. The technology mostly exists. It is a building story — wires, planning rows, supply chains, training colleges — and building is the thing modern Britain finds hardest. Whether 2040 looks like the day above depends on almost nothing invented after today.
Figure 03

Three things that could make this wrong

The build rate
The unfinished build-out
Everything above assumes the 2030s build at a pace the 2020s have not yet proven. If transmission slips and the workforce gap stays red, 2040 looks like 2032's plans, delayed — gas hangs on, bills stay hostage to it, and the constraint bill keeps growing.
The demand surprise
AI changed the forecast once already
Five years ago no forecast had AI demand in it. Something else will do the same to today's forecasts — bigger AI, new industries, electrified everything arriving faster or slower than planned. Double-by-2050 is the planning assumption, not a promise.
The politics
The 60-year asset problem
Grids are built across decades; governments change across years. A sustained political reversal — on targets, on planning reform, on who pays — would slow everything in this series. The physics is settled. The consensus is not guaranteed.
