Twenty years down, then the turn

LED bulbs, efficient appliances and deindustrialisation pushed UK electricity demand down for two decades — quietly cancelling out every new gadget. That era just ended. EVs, heat pumps and data centres are now pulling the line upward, and every forecast says it keeps climbing for the rest of our lives.

The honest framing
An individual's digital life is a rounding error next to their kettle and shower. The demand story is not you — it is the data centres themselves: training runs measured in gigawatt-hours, inference at planetary scale, and campuses requesting grid connections the size of towns. Your queries are the visible tip of an industrial iceberg.
The collision
Britain is adding its biggest new source of demand at exactly the moment it is rebuilding the entire supply side. It is renovating the kitchen during the dinner rush — and the new diners have arrived early, asking for grid connections measured in hundreds of megawatts.
Figure 03

Where the campuses are landing

Data centres cluster where fibre, land and power meet. The historic cluster hugs west London — which is exactly where the grid is most congested. The new wave is being steered toward AI Growth Zones with power to spare.

The Slough corridor
West London · The historic cluster
Europe's densest data centre cluster grew along the M4 for the fibre and the proximity to the City. It grew so fast that in 2022 grid capacity constraints in west London made national news — new housing and new data centres competing for the same substations. The queue reforms this series covered exist partly because of this corridor.
The AI Growth Zones
Culham, Teesside, Scotland · The new wave
Government-designated zones offering what AI campuses actually need: land, planning support and — above all — grid connections. The first, at Culham in Oxfordshire, sits next to the UK's fusion research campus. The pattern is deliberate: take the demand to where the power is, rather than reinforcing the grid to where the demand wants to be.
The Scottish logic
Following the wind north
Scotland generates far more wind power than it can use or export south — the constraint problem of Post 9. A data centre next to a Scottish wind farm soaks up power that would otherwise be curtailed, needs no new north-south transmission, and runs on some of the cleanest electricity in Europe. Expect more of this.
