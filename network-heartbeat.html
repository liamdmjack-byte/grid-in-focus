System frequency
50.00 Hz
The grid is healthy. Frequency sits in the green band because, at this exact moment, generation and demand are balanced to within fractions of a percent — and they have to be, every second, forever.
The synchronised machine
Every generator from Cornwall to Caithness spins in lockstep, fifty times a second — one nationwide machine with millions of moving parts. Your kettle is synchronised with every other kettle in Britain. Frequency is the machine's pulse: when it drops, somewhere, supply has just failed to meet demand.

Anatomy of a blackout — 9 August 2019, minute by minute

A Friday teatime in summer. Lightning hits a transmission line in Hertfordshire — routine, handled in milliseconds. But two large generators react badly and trip within seconds of each other. What follows is the grid's immune system working exactly as designed, and a country discovering what 48.8 Hz feels like.

Why the trains made it worse
The power cut lasted under an hour. The chaos lasted all day — because around 60 trains shut themselves down when frequency dipped, and half of them needed a technician to physically visit and reboot them. The grid recovered in 45 minutes; the railway didn't. The lasting lesson of 2019 was less about generation and more about everything plugged into it.

Who catches a falling grid

Defence in depth, ranked by speed. The deep change underway: the old grid's steadiness came free, from the sheer spinning mass of coal and gas turbines. As they retire, that inertia leaves with them — and batteries are being paid to replace physics with electronics.

Inertia — instant, automatic
The spinning mass
Hundreds of tonnes of steel spinning at 3,000 rpm in every conventional power station resist any change in speed — pure physics, no control system needed. It does not stop a fall; it slows one, buying seconds for everything else. Wind and solar contribute almost none, which is why a renewables grid needs the rest of this list more than ever.
Batteries — under a second
The new reflexes
Grid batteries watch the frequency and inject full power in a few hundred milliseconds — faster than anything in grid history. Services with names like Dynamic Containment barely existed in 2019; they are now the front line, and a major reason the fleet from Post 13 earns money even when it isn't trading energy.
Dinorwig — twelve seconds
The mountain, again
The Electric Mountain from the World Cup Special: 0 to 1,320 MW in around 12 seconds, on standby with turbines spinning in air for exactly these moments. Pumped hydro is the heavyweight responder — slower than batteries, but with vastly more behind it.
Load shedding — the last resort
What happened in 2019
If frequency ever reaches 48.8 Hz, relays in substations across the country automatically disconnect blocks of demand — no human in the loop, no choosing who. Brutal, indiscriminate, and the reason a bad afternoon in 2019 did not become a national blackout. It has triggered exactly once in modern memory.
