The whole argument in one sentence
A heat pump gives you roughly three units of heat per unit of electricity. Electricity costs roughly four times more than gas per unit. The entire national heat pump debate is the gap between those two numbers — and that gap is a policy choice, not physics.

Why the same heat pump pays in Oslo and struggles in Oldham

The electricity-to-gas price ratio varies wildly between countries — mostly because of where each chooses to load its policy costs. Britain stacks them on electricity. Where the bar sits below a heat pump's efficiency, going electric saves money automatically.

The quiet decision that would change everything
Most green levies and social policy costs sit on electricity bills, not gas — a legacy of when electricity was the dirty fuel. Moving them to gas or general taxation would cut the ratio toward 3:1 and make heat pumps clearly cheaper to run for most homes overnight. It has been reviewed, debated and deferred for years. It remains the single biggest lever nobody has pulled.
